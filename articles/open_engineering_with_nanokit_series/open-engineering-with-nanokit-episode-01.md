## Episode 1: The Blank Canvas

Every website has a moment before the first pixel goes down, when the only asset that exists is a small black logo and a conviction that it should stay exactly as clean as it is. That is where Open Engineering starts with Nano Kit: not with a template to fill in, but with a canvas that refuses to fight the identity already sitting on the desk.

### SIPOC

| Supplier | Input | Process | Output | Customer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brand owner | The Open Engineering logo, black-on-white identity | Choose a builder that will not force a heavy visual template over it | A design system that leaves the mark alone | Site visitors, future contributors |
| Nano Kit | Style range from minimal to maximal, corporate to creative | Select a minimal, engineering-grade style rather than a decorative one | A restrained visual baseline for every page that follows | Homepage, documentation, registry |
| Ecosystem architect | The four architectural dimensions already defined in GitHub | Decide the site should mirror the ecosystem, not invent a new one | A structural brief for the pages to come | Nano Kit generation step (Episode 2) |

### Why heavy templates were never the plan

Nano Kit markets itself around synth-powered, retro-futuristic style options that swing from minimal to maximal, corporate to creative, all powered by AI rather than a fixed template gallery. For Open Engineering, that range matters less for its maximal end and more because it means minimal is a first-class choice, not a compromise. The black logo can stay the loudest thing on the page.

### A canvas that matches the architecture, not the other way around

The temptation with any site builder is to let its defaults dictate the structure. Here the direction runs the other way: the four architectural dimensions, Platform, Architecture, Operating Systems, Applications, were already settled before a single Nano Kit page existed. The canvas is blank, but the floor plan for what goes on it is not.

Episode 2 picks up where the canvas ends and the first real page begins: turning that architecture into the words Nano Kit's AI needs to hear.
